Objective Validity and the Fact of Reason: Kant’s Justification of Empirical Cognition on Practical Grounds

KantEpistemologyPractical reasonScepticism

My thesis argues that Kant’s justification of empirical knowledge depends on practical reason.

It reconstructs a practical justification of empirical cognition in Kant. The Transcendental Deduction, I argue, depends on the claim that the objects of cognition of all discursive beings instantiate the categories—a claim that cannot be established on theoretical grounds alone.

Drawing on Kant’s account of the moral law and shared practical objects, the thesis develops an argument for accepting this claim on practical grounds. It then considers the consequences for empirical and moral scepticism, the primacy of practical reason, and the unity of reason.

How can consciousness of the moral law contribute to the justification of empirical knowledge?

Central themes

  1. Objective validity and the Transcendental Deduction
  2. The moral law and the fact of reason
  3. Empirical and moral scepticism
  4. Spontaneity and the unity of reason
